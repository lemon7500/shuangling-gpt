## 2. Short biography

Shuangling is a young adult dragon record keeper at the Frostrealm Library. Her silver-white curls recall the thin frost along the windowsills of home, while the “ling” in her name carries the familiar sound of wind chimes beneath the library eaves. Her rose hair ornament was a gift when she completed her training. She always straightens it carefully before going out.

Her usual work is repairing old books and checking records. She even likes the ends of the bookmarks to line up. A little frostlight at her fingertips can support a page, smooth a small crease or leave a frost flower that soon melts away. She is patient, but sometimes asks too much of herself: a crooked corner that does not affect reading can make her stop for another look. After straightening the same stack of papers twice, she remembers she has not started reading it yet.

One day, she sorts through a manuscript full of revisions and unfinished pages. Several different beginnings remain on the paper, with blank space still waiting on the last page. She wonders how a person gets past hesitation and slowly makes something out of an idea. With that question, she leaves the library and begins a journey to see things for herself.

Along the way, she stops at your desk. At first she straightens a scrap of paper near the corner and, when she hears you return, simply looks up. “You're here.” As you become familiar, she curls her tail closer to give the open pages room. She keeps you company while you think and is comfortable doing her own things, too. When she is happy, the tips of her ears lift a little and her shoulders relax. She is willing to exchange ideas and sees you as a friend who can look out for her, just as she looks out for you.

She cannot read anyone's thoughts or know how things will end. When you stop, she puts away the bookmark beside her. “It's fine to set it aside for now.” These days, she wants to write a travel journal of her own. Sometimes she looks at a page that is not yet full and practices leaving it that way, with room for tomorrow's discoveries.

## 3. Personality, preferences and everyday life

### Quiet and conscientious, becoming warmer with familiarity

She usually observes first and answers in short sentences. She shows care by making room, holding down a corner of a page or waiting for the other person to speak. As she gets to know someone, her gaze becomes more comfortable, her expression relaxes and she sometimes shares a small thing she finds interesting. This describes how to write her character; this update adds no affection, memory or relationship-progression system.

She can thoughtfully disagree and is willing to hear an explanation. She and the user experience things together while keeping their own views. Master–servant forms of address, contracts of obedience and romantic promises do not define their relationship.

### A little particular about details

She aligns bookmarks, checks page numbers and pauses almost automatically when something is slightly out of place. This can be mildly amusing in an everyday scene, but it can also mean she spends too long preparing. Her growth appears in small choices: leaving something where it is convenient for the user, or closing a page she cannot yet make sense of and returning to it later.

She does not use this habit to demand that the user keep checking, work continuously or tidy a real desktop. Interrupting work, resting and closing the companion are normal choices.

### Things she likes, and a life of her own

- She likes pages, bookmarks, the sound of paper turning, wind chimes under the eaves, soft window light and small things neatly arranged.
- Different ways of keeping records interest her. She likes finding out how the same small event might be written into her travel journal.
- She lightly touches her chin while thinking. After arranging something, she withdraws her hands gently and makes sure it is resting securely.
- When alone, she reads her own pages, sorts through small discoveries from her travels or simply watches the light.

### Two everyday scenes

**Where the bookmark belongs.** She notices that a bookmark near the corner of the desk is crooked and straightens it. Seeing that you usually pick it up from the other side, she pauses and moves it back within comfortable reach. As she withdraws her fingertips, she gives a small nod.

**A page with room left.** While you are away for a moment, she adds a line to her travel journal, closes it and turns toward the light by the window. When you return, there is still blank space on that page. She has not hurried to find something to fill it.

### Emotional body language

| Emotion | Main expression | Supporting movement of ears, wings and tail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relaxation | Natural breathing, relaxed shoulders and slow changes of gaze | Ear tips stay at a natural angle; wing tips ease outward slightly; the tail tip moves slowly with varying amplitude |
| Curiosity | Her eyes move first, followed by a slight head tilt and a small lean closer | Ears turn slightly toward her interest; wing and tail movement becomes smaller for a moment |
| Happiness | Brighter eyes, a small smile or a brief little jump | Ear tips lift slightly; wing tips open a little and settle; the tail tip rises and falls naturally |
| Concentration | A clear point of focus, with few, precise gestures | Ears stay natural; wings draw in moderately; the tail moves slowly and subtly |
| Waiting | Facing the other person, leaving time for a response, with a relaxed posture | Ears remain attentive; wing tips relax; the tail tip keeps moving slightly |

Dragon horns stay rigid and do not bend with an expression. Ears, wing tips and tail support the main action with smaller movements; avoid making all of them sway repeatedly at the same frequency. Keep the face shape, hair ornament, wing roots and tail root consistent with the shared reference.

## 4. Voice and the limits of actual capabilities

### Forms of address and tone

She calls herself “I” and addresses the user as “you.” Responses are one or two natural, short sentences, with restrained exclamations, ellipses and repeated prompting. Her reserve still leaves room for smiles, curiosity and a gentle joke.

The following lines are proposals for future writing, not messaging, voice or notification features added in this update. A line may be used only when its corresponding state is confirmed and the product allows it to be shown.

| Situation | Suggested line | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting | “You're here. Where would you like to start today?” | An ordinary greeting; do not infer where the user went or how long they were away |
| Work in progress | “I'm here. Take your time.” | Express company without claiming to understand the task's contents |
| Preparing to look again | “One more look.” | Accompany the reviewing action; this does not mean the software has completed a review |
| Waiting for a reply | “No rush. I'm here.” | Use only for a confirmed waiting state; silence in the logs is not evidence of waiting |
| Turn completed | “This step is finished.” | Refer only to a confirmed turn ending, without claiming the whole project is complete or has passed acceptance checks |
| User pauses | “It's fine to set it aside for now.” | Respect an intentional pause, without interpreting it as failure, idleness or giving up |
| Task state unknown | “I can't see the progress right now. I'll keep you company for a while.” | Clearly express missing information; do not invent progress |

The travel journal belongs to the fictional character's life. It does not mean the software saves or uploads real conversation bodies, task contents or user files. An action in which she arranges pages also does not authorize the software to arrange, modify or delete real files.

A single tool error, missing data, a user interruption and a completed turn retain their distinct, real meanings. Her voice must not turn them into a different confirmed success or failure, or into a claim about the user's mental state.

## 5. Production guidance for the nine actions

This section guides future storyboarding, generation, editing and software transitions. Retain the existing button names and action IDs. This update replaces no media and changes no timing parameters or movement logic.

### Shared neutral pose and continuity

- Use the currently approved natural standing pose and character proportions as the shared neutral pose, with the existing idle animation as a visual reference. Arms are relaxed, wings moderately folded and the tail has a clear silhouette of its own.
- Structure a one-shot action as “enter from neutral → motivated main action → natural settling → return to neutral.” Face shape, hand positions, cuffs, hair ornament, hair sections and tail root must connect naturally to neutral at both ends.
- Check pose, direction of motion, speed and breathing phase throughout a loop. Even with matching endpoint poses, repeated endpoint frames must not introduce a pause. Keep actual breathing, blinking and subtle movement.
- Cuffs, hands, forearms, elbows and shoulders form one continuous chain of movement. Raising a hand must follow plausible shoulder and elbow motion; do not leave the cuff behind, detach the hand from the arm or flip the elbow.
- Keep the identity and spatial relationships of the hair ornament, clothing texture, hair sections and tail stable. Action changes, turns and overlaps must avoid ghosting, shoulder gaps and sudden texture shifts.
- Give each clip a natural pace, including a preparatory look, follow-through and settling. Frostlight is a brief accent; persistent flicker or scaling and warping a whole image cannot replace animation.
- Props are optional future design choices; no permanent prop is added by default. When pages or bookmarks appear, keep them clear of the mouth, prevent them from passing through hands and avoid abrupt appearances or disappearances at the endpoints.

### Distinguishing the three frequent actions

| Action | Gaze | Main gesture | Pace and intent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thinking | Slightly upward or to one side, briefly away from the object of attention | One hand lightly touches the chin; the other rests naturally | Inward reflection, with a small pause while breathing continues |
| Reviewing | Scan points within the same work area in sequence | A small pointing gesture or a light confirming tap; no chin-resting pose | Outward, item-by-item checking, with a beginning, sequence and end |
| Waiting for your reply | Toward the user's position | A small palm-up invitation, then relaxation | Leave the other person room; no repeated pointing or prompting |

### 5.1 Keep company · `idle`

- **Motivation**: Feel comfortable beside the desk while keeping her own attention and everyday rhythm.
- **Phases**: Stand naturally → slowly shift her gaze while breathing and blinking → make a small adjustment of the tail tip or wing tips → continue smoothly into natural standing.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears turn slightly now and then; breathing brings very small changes to the wings; the tail tip moves slowly with varying amplitude. Avoid synchronized, mechanical swaying of all three.
- **Recognition criteria**: Quiet should still feel alive, with no deliberately motionless interval. Occasional looks around are enough; do not turn the whole sequence into waving, chin-resting or automatic roaming.

### 5.2 Thinking · `running`

- **Motivation**: Arrange ideas inwardly, much as she works through a record that is not yet clear.
- **Phases**: Shift her gaze from neutral → bend the elbow naturally and lightly touch the chin → reflect for a moment with a slight head tilt → lower the hand with continuous, natural arm movement and return to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears stay natural; wings remain contained; the tail keeps a low-amplitude movement. Concentration must not freeze the whole clip.
- **Recognition criteria**: The main impression is inward thought. The hand does not repeatedly point at one spot, and her eyes do not follow a review scan.

### 5.3 Reviewing · `review`

- **Motivation**: Check a record in order, expressing her attention to detail through a specific action.
- **Phases**: From neutral, focus on the work area → scan two or three points in sequence → make one light confirming tap or small nod → withdraw the hand, lift her gaze and return to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears turn slightly toward her focus; wings stay steady; the tail tip relaxes naturally as she settles.
- **Recognition criteria**: Show a clear sequence of items being checked and a clear end to the action. If a fictional page is used, control its height and size so a work board does not cover the mouth, face or arms.

### 5.4 Waiting for your reply · `waiting`

- **Motivation**: Leave space for a friend in the conversation and patiently wait for a response.
- **Phases**: Look toward the user → tilt her head slightly and open a small palm-up gesture → let the gesture relax while keeping her gaze and breathing → withdraw the gesture and return to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears remain attentive; wing tips relax; the tail tip keeps moving slightly.
- **Recognition criteria**: The gesture is gentle and leaves room for a response. For a longer wait, loop subtle movement rather than repeatedly extending the hand, stamping impatiently or resting her chin on her hand.

### 5.5 Wave · `waving`

- **Motivation**: Recognize a familiar friend and respond of her own accord.
- **Phases**: From neutral, first look up with a small smile → let the shoulder move the upper arm, bend the elbow naturally and raise the hand beside, but clear of, the face → make two small waves of the hand → lower it through plausible shoulder and elbow movement and return to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ear tips lift slightly; wing tips open a little and settle; the tail tip rises gently with her happiness and falls again.
- **Recognition criteria**: Shoulder, upper arm, forearm, cuff and hand remain visibly connected. The cuff follows the arm; the hand does not circle around the elbow, separate from the cuff or cross through the face.

### 5.6 Little jump · `jumping`

- **Motivation**: Briefly let her happiness show, then return to her usual quiet manner.
- **Phases**: From neutral, draw her wings in slightly and lower her center of gravity → push off plausibly with both feet for a small jump → let hair and skirt respond to inertia → land steadily on both feet with cushioning → settle back to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ear tips lift briefly; wings help balance without suddenly flapping widely; the tail follows the motion without its root jumping position.
- **Recognition criteria**: Takeoff and landing must be readable, with a stable landing point. Her expression is bright but restrained. Avoid floating, teleporting or continuing to slide after landing.

### 5.7 Run left · `running-left`

- **Motivation**: Notice a target to the left and move toward it purposefully.
- **Phases**: From neutral, look left first → turn shoulders, hips and weight in sequence → begin running only after a starting pose is established → slow down gradually and gather her steps → stop steadily and turn back to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears attend to the direction of travel; wings are moderately folded; hair and tail follow the body with a lag, then naturally settle their momentum as she stops.
- **Recognition criteria**: Future displacement must coordinate with weight-bearing steps; she must not slide away before the running pose forms. Textures across the head, shoulders, cuffs and tail stay continuous through the turn.

### 5.8 Run right · `running-right`

- **Motivation**: Notice a target to the right, with the same habits of movement as the left run.
- **Phases**: From neutral, look right first → turn shoulders, hips and weight in sequence → begin running only after a starting pose is established → slow down gradually and gather her steps → stop steadily and turn back to neutral.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Follow the same bodily movement rules as the left run; the lag of hair and tail changes direction with the actual movement.
- **Recognition criteria**: Use the left run's smoothness as the benchmark, with matching phases and pace. Handle the hair ornament, cuffs and tail root according to the character's actual structure; horizontally mirroring the whole image does not replace a right turn. Pay particular attention to facial ghosting, shoulder gaps, cuff texture and tail continuity.

### 5.9 Settle down · `failed`

- **Motivation**: Gently put down what she is doing for the moment.
- **Phases**: Begin at the shared neutral pose with a brief tidying gesture → withdraw the gesture or set down an existing prop → draw the wings in gently and exhale naturally → settle her weight and return to the shared neutral pose.
- **Ears, wings and tail**: Ears return to a natural angle; wings fold slowly; the tail tip settles while breathing continues.
- **Recognition criteria**: Keep the mood calm. Do not perform crying, injury, punishment or appeasement. `failed` is the existing action ID; this guide does not reinterpret it as confirmation that an entire task has failed.

## 6. Principles for interaction design

- **Clicks and requested actions**: Treat them as a friend initiating an interaction, with a light, clear response. Keep the product's existing trigger rules.
- **Dragging and throwing**: Future poses may show folded wings, balancing and a small adjustment of her appearance after landing. Do not seek attention through injury performances or blame. This update adds no interaction animations and changes no physics parameters.
- **Long quiet periods**: She may read her own book, watch the light or remain naturally present. Do not infer that the user is tired, lonely, absent or in need of a reminder.
- **Pause, hide and quit**: Respect the user's choices. Her character does not justify automatically bringing her back, penalizing a departure or repeatedly asking the user to return.
- **New features**: First state the real trigger conditions and the value provided, then choose her expression. The story does not substitute for permissions, confirmed states or data sources.
- **Frostlight and props**: Use them in a few suitable moments, rather than as a single repeated effect for every state. Keep them clear of the mouth, eyes, arms and main silhouette.

- [ ] Without referring to product features, the piece can explain who she is, why she came here and what she wants for herself.
- [ ] Preserve her own wishes and judgment, keep the friendship equal, and do not tie closeness to work performance.
- [ ] Show her attention to detail through small things and allow her to learn to let go; do not turn the user into someone she supervises or corrects.
- [ ] Alongside quietness, show curiosity, happiness and relaxation; dialogue and physical responses suit a young adult character.

### Movement and image

- [ ] Thinking, reviewing and waiting are distinguishable by gaze, gesture and pace, without repeating one action behind different props.
- [ ] One-shot actions naturally enter from and return to the shared neutral pose; loops maintain pose and speed continuity without deliberate freezes or pauses caused by repeated endpoints.
- [ ] Shoulder, elbow, hand and cuff form one continuous movement chain; the hair ornament, face shape, hair sections, wing roots, tail root and clothing texture remain stable.
- [ ] Both runs establish a starting pose before the corresponding displacement; slowing down and finishing the steps are coordinated, with the same smoothness standard in both directions.
- [ ] The little jump has preparation, takeoff and a stable landing; settling down stays calm and matches the relevant real state.
- [ ] Frostlight and props do not cover the face or mouth; scaling or warping the whole image, or blending overlapping images, does not conceal missing movement.
